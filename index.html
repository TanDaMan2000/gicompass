GastroLens: live first version Open Me: community screening campaign

Building better ways to understand and act on GI health.

GastroCompass is a research-driven initiative made of two connected parts: GastroLens, a non-diagnostic tool for organizing colorectal cancer symptom patterns, and Open Me, a community campaign that turns screening awareness into real conversations.

01 — Origin

It started with a question.

GastroCompass began with a simple, unresolved observation: colorectal cancer was showing up in people who did not fit the picture most of us have of who gets it. Seeing that up close, in a rural Texas community, was the moment the project's founder started asking why.

Not with a plan to build a product. With curiosity. Why do symptoms get dismissed? Why does screening awareness lag behind the actual risk? What would it take to help people take a warning sign seriously before it becomes a late diagnosis?

Those questions are what turned into a research project, and eventually, into two different kinds of answers.

02 — Ideation & Research

The questions led to research, not a rush to build.

Before any code was written, the work was reading, questioning, and testing whether the idea held up. That included a completed research paper, a feasibility analysis grounded in NHANES public-health data, and three empirical analyses used to stress-test the concept before committing to a build.

03 — GastroLens

A research-mode tool for symptom patterns, not a diagnosis.

GastroLens is a live first-version tool that helps people organize persistent colorectal cancer warning signs, such as rectal bleeding, bowel changes, and their duration, alongside risk context like age and family history. It turns scattered symptom history into one clear, structured summary.

GastroLens is informational and non-diagnostic. It is not clinically validated and is built to support an earlier conversation with a physician, not replace one.

04 — The Realization

GastroLens could organize a symptom pattern. It could not make someone open it in the first place.

A tool only helps the people who already know to look for it. Building GastroLens made that limitation obvious: the harder problem wasn't the technology, it was awareness. That realization is what led to Open Me.

05 — Open Me

Awareness, off the screen and into everyday moments.

Open Me is a community screening-awareness campaign. Folded cards go into local takeout orders, each one built around a single message: most adults at average risk should start colorectal cancer screening at 45, and it often causes no early symptoms at all.

07 — Impact & What's Next

Where things stand, and where they're going.

What has happened

  • Research foundation. A completed paper, an NHANES-grounded feasibility analysis, and three empirical analyses behind the concept.
  • GastroLens, live. A first-version, non-diagnostic symptom-pattern tool, launched from this site.
  • Open Me cards, designed and printed. Folded takeout cards built for local restaurant distribution and QR-linked outreach.
  • A working pledge. A real doctor-talk commitment, collected through Open Me, connected to a live database.

What's next

  • More restaurant partners. Expanding where Open Me cards reach people, one neighborhood at a time.
  • Continued GastroLens development. Refining the symptom-first logic while keeping it explicitly non-diagnostic.
  • Imaging, as future work. An imaging layer stays on the roadmap until it can be trained and validated on clinically meaningful data.
  • More research. Ongoing analysis to keep the project's claims grounded in evidence, not marketing.
